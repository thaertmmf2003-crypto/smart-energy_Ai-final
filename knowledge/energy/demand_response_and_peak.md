# Demand response and peak demand management

## What peak demand is
Peak demand is the highest rate of electricity use (kW) over a billing or
operating interval. Utilities and grid operators size infrastructure for peaks,
so many tariffs charge separately for the peak (a demand charge) in addition to
the energy used (kWh). A single short peak can set the demand charge for a whole
billing period.

## Energy versus demand
- Energy (kWh) is the total amount used over time.
- Demand (kW) is the instantaneous rate of use.
Shifting a load to a later hour may leave total energy almost unchanged while
cutting the peak substantially. Demand response is mostly about demand, not
energy.

## Demand response
Demand response means changing consumption in response to a signal: a price
change, a grid stress warning, or an agreed limit on grid import. Typical
building measures are:
- Temporary HVAC setpoint adjustment or pre-cooling
- Shifting flexible loads such as EV charging, water heating or batch processes
- Discharging on-site battery storage during the peak window
- Dimming or switching off non-essential lighting

## Grid import limits
A site may have a contractual or physical limit on how much power it can import.
As forecast demand approaches that limit, the operator has less headroom and the
risk of penalties or protective tripping grows. Acting on a forecast, before the
peak arrives, is more effective than reacting once the limit is reached.

## Rebound effects
Many demand-response measures postpone consumption rather than remove it.
Deferred EV charging, recovering zone temperatures and recharging batteries all
add load after the event. Plan the recovery period so the rebound does not create
a new peak.

## Coordinating several measures
Combining measures can deliver a larger reduction than any single one, at the
cost of more operational disruption and more things that can underperform.
A good strategy uses the least disruptive measure that achieves the required
reduction, and escalates only when severity justifies it.
