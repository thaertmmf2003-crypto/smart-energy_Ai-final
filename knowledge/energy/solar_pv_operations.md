# Solar PV generation and operation

## How output is determined
A photovoltaic array's output depends mainly on the solar irradiance reaching the
panels (W/m²), the panel temperature, and system losses. Module ratings are given
at Standard Test Conditions: 1000 W/m² irradiance and 25 °C cell temperature.
Real output is almost always below the nameplate rating.

## Temperature effect
Crystalline silicon modules lose output as they heat up. A typical power
temperature coefficient is around -0.3 to -0.5 % per °C above 25 °C cell
temperature. On hot, clear days panels can run far above ambient temperature,
so peak irradiance does not always mean peak efficiency.

## Daily and seasonal profile
Output follows the sun: zero at night, rising through the morning and peaking
around solar noon. Summer days give longer and higher production curves than
winter days. Clouds, dust and shading cause short-term drops.

## Performance ratio
The performance ratio compares actual energy produced with the energy expected
from the measured irradiance and the array rating. Well-maintained systems
commonly operate somewhere around 0.75 to 0.85. A sustained drop points to
soiling, shading, inverter faults or failed strings.

## Common causes of underperformance
- Dust and soiling, which matter especially in dry, dusty climates
- Partial shading from new structures, trees or equipment
- Inverter faults, clipping or tripping
- String or connector failures
- High module temperatures

## Solar and building load
On-site solar reduces net import from the grid while it is producing. Because
building peaks often occur in the afternoon, solar can help at peak time, but
late-afternoon peaks may coincide with falling solar output. Batteries and load
shifting can cover that gap.
