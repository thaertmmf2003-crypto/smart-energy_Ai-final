# Building energy efficiency concepts

## Baseline and anomaly
An energy baseline is the consumption expected for a building under given
conditions: time of day, day of week, weather and occupancy. Forecasting models
learn this baseline from history. An energy anomaly is a period where actual
consumption differs significantly from the baseline, in either direction.

## Common causes of anomalies
- Equipment left running outside scheduled hours (after-hours HVAC, lighting)
- Control faults: stuck dampers or valves, failed sensors, overridden schedules
- Simultaneous heating and cooling in the same zone
- Unusual occupancy or events not reflected in the schedule
- Metering or data errors, which should be ruled out before acting
A consumption level far below forecast can also be an anomaly, for example a
failed meter or equipment that did not start.

## Investigating an anomaly
1. Confirm the reading is real: check the meter and data quality.
2. Compare end uses (HVAC, lighting, plug loads, EV) with their usual profile.
3. Check occupancy against the schedule.
4. Check weather: unusual heat or cold explains many deviations.
5. Look at the history to see whether the deviation is new or recurring.

## Energy use intensity
Energy use intensity (EUI) is annual energy divided by floor area (kWh/m²/year).
It lets buildings of different sizes be compared. Laboratories usually have much
higher EUI than offices or classrooms because of ventilation and equipment loads.

## Efficiency versus flexibility
Efficiency measures reduce energy permanently (better insulation, efficient
equipment, correct schedules). Flexibility measures move or trim demand when it
matters most (demand response, storage). A well-run building needs both.

## Measurement and verification
Savings cannot be metered directly; they are the difference between what was
used and what would have been used without the measure. Verification therefore
compares observed consumption with an adjusted baseline or forecast, and reports
the achieved saving against the expected saving.
